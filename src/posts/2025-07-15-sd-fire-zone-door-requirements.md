San Diego County has one of the highest concentrations of Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) designated properties in California. If your home sits in a Very High or High Fire Hazard Severity Zone — which includes large swaths of Rancho Santa Fe, Poway, Santee, El Cajon, Alpine, and parts of Escondido — your entry door options are governed by California Building Code Chapter 7A and local fire overlay requirements.

This catches a lot of homeowners and even some contractors off guard, especially on remodels where the permit triggers a full code review.

## What the Code Actually Requires

For homes in designated fire hazard zones, exterior doors must meet one of the following:

- **Solid wood or solid steel** with no glass, or
- **Fire-rated glazing** in any glass panels or sidelites (minimum 20-minute rating in most jurisdictions)
- **Fiberglass doors with fire-rated construction** — this is where most of the current product development is happening

The critical detail: standard decorative glass sidelites — the kind you see flanking thousands of San Diego entry doors — do not meet WUI requirements unless they use fire-rated glazing. This is a common code violation on unpermitted remodels.

## What That Rules Out

In a WUI zone, you generally cannot use:

- Standard wood doors with decorative glass lites (unless fire-rated glass is specified)
- Most decorative wrought iron and glass assemblies
- Standard vinyl doors (vinyl doesn't meet WUI requirements at all)

**Steel doors** — including utility and secondary entry doors — almost always meet fire requirements by default, but verify the specific rating with the manufacturer.

## Getting Your Project Approved

The single most important step: pull your parcel's fire hazard severity zone designation before speccing any door. You can look it up at the CAL FIRE FHSZ viewer online. If you're in a Very High zone, bring the door spec sheet to your building department early — don't wait until plan check.

San Diego's building departments have been increasingly rigorous about WUI compliance on remodels since the 2018 and 2021 fire seasons. What slipped through on a permit five years ago may not today.
