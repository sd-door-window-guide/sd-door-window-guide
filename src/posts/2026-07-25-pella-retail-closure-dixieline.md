Pella has closed its Southern California retail showroom locations, marking a significant shift in how the brand will be accessed in this market going forward. If you've worked with Pella through their direct retail channel — or have clients who have — here's what the transition looks like on the ground.

## What Changed

Pella's company-owned retail stores in Southern California are closed. This affects the direct-to-consumer and custom sales channel that Pella operated regionally, where homeowners and contractors could visit a showroom, work with a Pella-employed sales consultant, and place orders directly through the manufacturer.

This wasn't a quiet wind-down. It was a real disruption for customers mid-project and for the Pella sales staff who built their careers around the brand.

## Where the Team Went

The good news for anyone who had an established relationship with a Pella consultant: most of Pella's former Southern California retail employees have been absorbed by Dixieline. Dixieline has taken on the custom Pella sales function in the So-Cal market, and much of the institutional knowledge and client relationship continuity has come with it.

If you were working with a specific Pella rep at one of the retail locations, there's a reasonable chance they're now at Dixieline and can pick up where you left off.

## How to Access Pella in Southern California Now

The channel has consolidated into two tracks:

**Custom and in-home sales** — now handled exclusively through Dixieline in the Southern California market. This covers the full Pella lineup including the Architect Series, Reserve, and Lifestyle Series custom configurations — the products most relevant to remodels, custom builds, and anything requiring non-stock sizing, finish customization, or professional installation.

**Lower-end vinyl options** — still available through big box retail channels (Home Depot carries a limited Pella vinyl selection). This covers entry-level stock-size product only and does not include the custom configuration, professional consultation, or in-home measurement services that the former retail locations provided.

For any project that requires custom sizing, specific finishes, divided lites, or professional installation — Dixieline is now the path in Southern California.

## What This Means for the Market

Pella pulling back from company-owned retail is part of a broader industry pattern of manufacturers leaning more heavily on established dealer networks rather than operating their own showroom infrastructure. Running retail in Southern California is expensive, and the economics of manufacturer-owned retail have been challenging across the building products category.

The transition to Dixieline as the primary channel actually makes sense for the professional market. Dixieline has deep relationships with San Diego's builder and remodeler community, carries multiple complementary product lines, and has the service infrastructure to support in-home measurement and installation coordination in a way that a standalone manufacturer showroom often struggles with.

For homeowners who previously preferred going direct to Pella retail, the adjustment is working with a dealer rather than a manufacturer-owned location. The product is the same — it's the front door that changed.

## For Contractors and Builders

If you've been quoting Pella through the retail channel, contact Dixieline to establish or update your trade account. The transition is still settling in, so the earlier you get in front of the right person there, the smoother your next Pella project will go.
